# Jurisdictional Definitions

A working glossary of the "jurisdictions" mapped by this project: named traps, chambers, and enclosures produced by late-stage systems (economic, familial, legal, bureaucratic, biopolitical) that constrain, extract from, or discipline the people caught inside them.

**How to use this document:** Each entry gets a rudimentary, deliberately general definition meant to hold up over time — not tied to one anecdote or news cycle. Treat the definitions as a first cut, not a final word. As you encounter new cases, sources, or sharper phrasing, edit the entry directly. The `Notes / Evidence / Updates` line under each one is there for exactly that — running notes, examples, citations, revisions — so this stays a living document instead of a fixed reference.

## 1. Precarity Trap under Late-Stage Neoliberal Atomization

**Core definition:** The condition in which individuals are stripped of collective structures (unions, extended family, stable community, guaranteed welfare) and made solely responsible for absorbing economic shocks, while the market that produces those shocks bears none of the cost. Atomization isn't incidental — it's the mechanism that makes precarity personal instead of political.

**Key mechanism:** Risk is privatized and individualized; solidarity infrastructure is dismantled or discouraged; instability is reframed as a personal failure of planning, resilience, or character.

## 2. Bureaucracy of the Micro-Domestic Panopticon

**Core definition:** A regime of surveillance and paperwork that extends state or institutional scrutiny into the smallest details of a household — chores, routines, spending, caregiving — so that domestic life itself becomes a site of monitoring and self-justification.

**Key mechanism:** Small-scale, everyday domestic behavior is rendered visible/auditable (via forms, case notes, means-testing, wellness checks, etc.), inducing constant self-policing in anticipation of inspection.

## 3. Biopolitical Panic Room of Pathologized Intimacy

**Core definition:** A retreat into diagnosis and clinical language as the only sanctioned way to name relational or emotional distress, such that intimacy itself becomes something to be managed, medicated, or treated rather than simply lived — with the "panic room" offering safety only by converting the relationship into a condition.

**Key mechanism:** Ordinary relational pain is reclassified as pathology; the vocabulary of therapy/diagnosis becomes the only legitimate channel for expressing need, narrowing what intimacy is allowed to look like.

## 4. Panoptic Poorhouse of Hereditary Containment

**Core definition:** A structure — economic, legal, or familial — that keeps poverty circulating within the same lineage across generations while making every member feel individually watched and individually blamed for not escaping it.

**Key mechanism:** Barriers to exit (debt, inheritance of obligation, lack of asset transfer) combine with visibility/judgment (means-testing, stigma, surveillance of "the poor") to make containment feel like personal failure rather than structural design.

## 5. The Ostracized Underworld Panopticon of Disenfranchised Specters

**Core definition:** The condition of people rendered socially invisible or "ghosted" by systems (undocumented status, criminal record, homelessness, estrangement) who are nonetheless still tracked, flagged, and surveilled by those same systems — visible to power, invisible to community.

**Key mechanism:** Formal disenfranchisement (no vote, no standing, no recognized voice) is paired with informal, often invisible tracking (databases, background checks, watch-lists), producing a class that is watched but not heard.

## 6. The Jurisprudence of Affective Usury: The Doctrine of Implied Quasi-Fiduciary Entrapment

**Core definition:** A conceptual legal framework describing how one party can extract ongoing emotional "interest payments" from another by implying a duty of care or loyalty that was never formally agreed to — trust is manufactured, then billed against indefinitely.

**Key mechanism:** An implied (never contracted) obligation of trust/loyalty is asserted after the fact; the "debt" is emotional rather than financial, but functions the same way usury does — extraction that compounds and is never considered fully repaid.

**Key mechanism:** Managerial rhetoric (empowerment, ownership, accountability) masks concentrated top-down authority; when harm occurs, responsibility is rhetorically shifted downward onto the subordinate party.

## 8. The Thermodynamics of the Affective Heat Sink: The Hydraulic Conduit of Tragic Trauma-Passing

**Core definition:** A metaphorical system in which one person (or role) absorbs and dissipates the excess emotional "heat" (distress, trauma, dysfunction) generated elsewhere, so the system as a whole can keep functioning — at the cost of that person's own equilibrium.

**Key mechanism:** Distress flows downhill, from those with more power/stability to those with less, along the path of least resistance (the family scapegoat, the junior employee, the eldest daughter); the sink absorbs but rarely gets to release in turn.

**Key mechanism:** Chronic stress/scarcity forces the body or system into a defensive crouch that is metabolically or structurally unsustainable, so "safety" is purchased by drawing down internal capital that isn't being replenished.

**Key mechanism:** Wealth and authority pass by inheritance rather than active stewardship or accountability; the people bearing the consequences of decisions rarely encounter the people (nominally) making them.

## 12. The Dissipative Slump-State Niche: The Pulsed-Burst Service Sink

**Core definition:** A structural role or labor niche defined by irregular, on-demand bursts of intense output followed by unpaid, unstructured idle time — energy demanded in spikes, with the "slump" periods absorbed entirely by the worker rather than compensated by the system.

**Key mechanism:** Common to gig/on-call/service labor: value is extracted in short high-intensity bursts (a shift, a rush, a callout), while the recovery time between bursts is externalized onto the worker as unpaid downtime.

## 13. The Panoptic Purgatory of the Standby Car: The Imperial Proving-Ground Trap

**Key mechanism:** Conditional, provisional status (probation, contractor status, immigration status, "at-will" standing) is maintained indefinitely; the proving never concludes because concluding it would require the system to extend real security.

**Key mechanism:** Compliance is measured in punctuality and clocked hours rather than outcomes; workers' internal/biological clocks are subordinated to externally imposed schedules, and deviation from the schedule is treated as the offense.

**Key mechanism:** Emotional labor is compulsory and one-directional (customer service, caregiving, "culture fit"); genuine affect is replaced by a fixed, surveilled performance of affect, and exhaustion accumulates precisely because the mask is never permitted to slip.

## 16. The Fugitive Nomad's Mirage: The Thermodynamic Escape-Vector of the Feral Threshold

**Core definition:** The recurring, often illusory promise that total exit from a system (dropping out, going off-grid, "starting over" elsewhere) will resolve what the system itself produced — an escape route that looks thermodynamically clean (all cost left behind) but usually just relocates or defers the cost.

**Key mechanism:** The appeal of a clean break obscures the fact that most systems' pressures follow the person past the threshold; "escape" reduces visible constraint without necessarily reducing the underlying extractive relationship.

**Key mechanism:** Kinship and regulation happen through direct, embodied, non-verbal relation (with land, animals, elements, cycles) rather than through documentation, contract, or hierarchy — offering renewal precisely because it resists being administered.

**Key mechanism:** Real kinship networks erode or are severed under surveillance/relocation/economic pressure; synthetic replacements are offered in their place but are structurally shallow — a well that looks full but doesn't actually draw from a real source.

**Core definition:** The romanticized figure of the wandering outsider who trades wisdom, healing, or insight for subsistence — a mirage in the sense that this archetype is often invoked to dignify economic precarity (no stable income, no institutional support) rather than to actually provide for the person living it.

**Key mechanism:** Cultural mythology (the wise wanderer, the healer who owns nothing) is used to reframe structural exclusion from stable livelihood as spiritual choice or calling, obscuring the lack of material support behind the archetype.

## 23. The Panoptic Domestic Purgatory: The Schmoozer's Domestic Pan-Carceral Cell

**Core definition:** A household or relational space that functions as a holding cell dressed up in sociability — where charm, small talk, and performed friendliness ("schmoozing") are the price of continued residence or belonging, and where surveillance of one's behavior never actually lifts despite the congenial surface.

**Key mechanism:** Social performance (being liked, being easy, being agreeable) becomes a survival requirement inside a space that is otherwise confining; the charm is not freely given warmth but a toll paid to avoid worse treatment.
